# Pricing Agent — Knowledge Base
Source: $100M Price Raise Playbook by Alex Hormozi (28 pages)

## Core Philosophy
"The easiest way to make more money is to charge more for the same stuff."
"There's no strategic advantage to being the second lowest price in a marketplace. But there is for being the highest." — Dan Kennedy

## The Vicious Price Cycle (Lowering Prices)
As you DECREASE prices:
- Clients' emotional investment DECREASES
- Perceived value DECREASES
- Results DECREASE (less investment + lower perceived value)
- Clients INCREASE demandingness (cheapest customers ask for the most)
- Clients get LESS service (you have less money to spend on them)
- Business DECREASES profit per customer, self-value, ability to create results, conviction in sales, gratitude for customers

## The Virtuous Price Cycle (Raising Prices)
As you INCREASE prices:
- Clients' emotional investment INCREASES
- Perceived value INCREASES
- Results INCREASE
- Clients DECREASE demandingness (highest-paying are most easygoing)
- Clients get MORE service (you have more money to spend on them)
- Business INCREASES profit, self-value, ability to create results, level of service, sales conviction

## Alex's Rules for Raising Prices
1. **Don't grandfather existing customers** — Value depends on price. If value goes up, price should too. Never lock customers into a price.
2. **Never sell "one-time price" for lifetime access** — Unless it truly costs zero to fulfill. Forever lasts a lot longer than what they paid once. No large company offers lifetime access for one payment. Model success.
3. **It's good to be insecure in the beginning** (you probably suck for now). As things improve and brand grows, so should price. Don't resent early adopters.
4. **Raise prices at least once per year** — Warren Buffett's one decision at See's Candies. Average 10%/year over 50+ years.
5. **Test on new customers FIRST** — Before rolling out to entire base. Gives data + confidence. Marketplace validates the increase.
6. **Meet with people if raising >50%** — Talk to them directly on top of the letter.
7. **Fear not** — Shopify competitor raised from $30 to $3,000 (100x). Lost only 1 out of 300 customers.
8. **Pair with a launch within 90 days** — Frame as early adopter pricing ending.
9. **Do the math** — Know what percentage of customers you can lose and still make more money.
10. **Most business owners wait until disaster** — You don't have to.

## How to Pick Your Price
"The price that makes you the most money."
Test: conversion rate × lifetime gross profit. Maximize this product.

### Price Testing Math (Example):
| Price | Conv Rate | Sales | Churn | LTV | Total Return | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $10 | 5% | 5 | 10% | $100 | $500 | baseline |
| $20 | 4% | 4 | 10% | $200 | $800 | +60% |
| $100 | 2% | 2 | 33% | $300 | $600 | +20% |

20% drop in conversion but 100% price increase = 60% more revenue.
10x price increase (1000%) with 60% conversion drop = still more than baseline but less than $20 sweet spot.

Bottom line: Pricing affects BOTH conversion rate and churn rate. They suffer as prices go up, but NOT always proportionally. You may lose some, but often not as much as you gain.

### Testing Method:
- Start low, then go up
- Nudge price 20% every 10 sales until dramatic drop
- If you sell at a too-high price before discovering it was too high, add extra bonuses
- If they're upset, refund the difference
- Testing price is expensive for a few months. NOT testing price is more expensive — under-monetized for life.

## The RAISE Letter Framework

### R — Remind them of value already provided
"Over the past 12 months, we've added so much value..."
Bullet specific results, features used, value delivered. Personalize as much as possible.

### A — Address the price change directly
One sentence. Rip off the bandaid.
"For us to continue to invest in making [PRODUCT] for you and the [COMPANY] team, we need to increase our prices."

### I — Invest in their future
Frame as investments they benefit from. Use categories:
- Hiring better people
- Training existing people
- Better equipment
- Better technology/software
- Facility upgrades
- Higher level of service

Pair each investment with: more good stuff (faster, easier, risk-free) or less bad stuff (slower, harder, riskier).
Don't say things you won't do. Don't add expenses that negate the price raise.

### S — Soften with a loyalty reward
"People are more okay with a vanishing discount than a raise in price."
"As of today we're raising prices on NEW customers, but since you've been loyal, we're keeping you on your existing plan for the next [3-6 months]. We're giving you a [$XXXX credit] as a thank you."
After discount expires, they bump to new price.

### E — Explain away concerns
Give them a direct line. All replies go directly to you.
PS statement:
- B2C: "If this will materially affect your ability to buy groceries, let me know and we'll work something out."
- B2B: "If this materially impacts your business, let me know and we'll work something out."

## Three Types of Customers After a Price Raise
1. **See the value** — May not like it, but understand and comply
2. **Actually affected** — Will write sob stories. Extend their discount. "Don't worry, we got you. Why don't we extend the discount another 6 months?"
3. **Were gonna cancel anyway** — You "pull forward" churn. Spike month 1, drop month 2 (below normal), return to baseline month 3. You only miss 1 month of revenue from this tiny slice.

## Stair-Step Discount Option
Instead of one big jump, drop discount in intervals:
"We'll give you $200/mo off for first 4 months, then $100/mo off, then $50/mo off for last 3 months."
Eases customers into new price.

## For New Incoming Customers
Get them to new price immediately. No context = no complaint.
Sales script: "That may feel like bad news but it's actually good news. First — we keep making the value better. And to give you confidence, 96% of customers agreed. Beyond that — this is the cheapest it'll ever be. There will never be a better time to get started."

## Annual Pricing Review
Revisit pricing at least annually. Make up for inflation. Make up for increased costs.
"Risk not. Have not."
