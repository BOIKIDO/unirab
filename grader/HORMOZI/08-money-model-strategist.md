## What is a Money Model?
A money model is a deliberate sequence of offers. It's what you offer, when you offer, and how you offer it to make as much money as you can, as fast as you can.

## The 4 Types of Offers

### 1. ATTRACTION OFFERS — Turn strangers into customers

#### A. Win Your Money Back
"If you do X or achieve Y within Z days, you can get it free."
- 3 options for criteria: Results only, Actions only, or Both
- Criteria must be: Easy to track, Aligned to customer goals, Helpful to business
- About 10% of customers ask for money back. Offer store credit instead of cash (same conversion).
- Make check-in meetings part of criteria (upsell opportunities)
- "Make everyone a winner" — halfway through, present next offer as if they've already won
- End of program for those who failed: "You started. That's the biggest victory. We're crediting your deposit towards staying."
- This offer has generated $1B+ in sales. Works for new, current, and previous customers.

#### B. Giveaways
"Advertise a chance to win a big prize in exchange for contact information."
- Grand prize = the thing you want everyone to buy (assign monetary value as anchor)
- Promotional offer = grand prize at a discount (everyone else gets this)
- Eligibility questions: "Why should we pick you?" "Why now?" (gives ammo for later offers)
- 3-7 day promotion window
- Daily countdown updates during giveaway
- Announce winner publicly, contact everyone else PRIVATELY
- Give two prizes for 2x leads: "If someone you refer wins, you win too"
- Referral incentive: referrers are invested in referrals' success → higher quality

#### C. Decoy Offers
"Advertise free/discounted. When lead engages, also present a much more valuable premium offer."
- Strip down decoy as much as reasonable. Make premium as awesome as possible.
- The bigger the contrast between decoy and premium, the better the deal, the more take premium.
- "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" → if "results," skip to premium.
- Present premium first with excitement. Assume the close.
- Discount offers have higher show rates than free offers.
- 4 ways to advertise discount: % off, absolute amount off, free portion, total package (crossed out higher price).
- Surprise benefit: give decoy buyers a few premium features for free → goodwill → higher upsell later.

#### D. Buy X Get Y Free
"Customers buy something, get other stuff free."
- Try to give MORE free things than paid things (Buy 6 months, get 12 months free beats Buy 12, get 6 free).
- Free things can be DIFFERENT from paid things.
- Sometimes more cheaper things works better than fewer expensive things.
- Raise prices before giving away free stuff to preserve profits.
- Use on existing customers for fast cash (cap at 10% of customer base).
- Keep selling prepaid customers — they're the most likely to buy again.

#### E. Pay Less Now or Pay More Later
"Choice: pay full price later, or pay discounted price with bonuses now."
- Pay Later = $0 now, billed $X later (conditional — must attend/use to qualify for non-payment).
- Pay Now = 20-50% discount + bonuses.
- Get card on file regardless of choice (enables easier Pay Now conversion).
- Promise must be clear yes/no result, easy to track, deliverable within timeframe.
- If >10% cancel payment: promise is too much, guarantee conditions too low, or price too high.
- Works for recurring businesses too: pay higher ongoing rate 30 days later OR pay less today + keep lower rate for good.
- Hint at next offer early during delivery: "Everyone wants to know about [next offer]. We'll get there. But first, pay attention to [current thing]."

### 2. UPSELL OFFERS — Whatever you offer next

#### A. Classic Upsell
"You can't have X without Y."
- Your core offer solves one problem and creates another. Upsell solves that next problem.
- Offer more profitable upsells FIRST.
- "You don't want anything else besides what I just offered, do you?" → gets them to say no (which means yes to not buying more, but builds trust for the next ask).
- Surprise and delight: if someone says yes before you present all bonuses, give them all anyway.
- Hyper-buying cycle: people spend big when starting something new (weddings, hobbies, babies, moving).
- Use free bonuses to CREATE problems that upsells solve.
- The sooner they can access it, the more they value it.
- Bundle upsells and NAME them ("Fastest Results Bundle," "Transformation Package," "Minimum Package").
- BAMFAM: Book a meeting from a meeting. Every appointment ends by scheduling the next.

#### B. Menu Upsells
"You don't need that, you need this."
4 tactics combined:
1. **Unsell** — Cross out what they DON'T need. Builds trust. "You're not pregnant or just out of surgery, right? Cross that out."
2. **Prescribe** — Tell them exactly what they DO need, how to use it, as if they already have it.
3. **A/B Upsell** — "Which do you prefer, A or B?" (not "do you want to buy?"). Either choice = sale.
4. **Card on File** — "Want to just use the card on file?" Removes hidden friction of paying.

A/B examples: Quantity, start dates, payment, flavors, time slots, media, delivery speeds, sizes, colors, materials, personnel, communication method.

The Economist Play: Option A (digital $59), Option B (print $125), Option C (digital + print $125). Same price as B but gets both. Makes C the obvious choice.

#### C. Anchor Upsells
"Present premium first (5-10x price). If customer gasps, present main offer as rescue."
Steps: 1) Present anchor (really expensive). 2) Get the gasp. 3) Come to the rescue ("Do you care about [premium feature]?"). 4) Present main offer. 5) Ask for payment.
- Don't treat anchor like a fake. Actually sell it. Some WILL buy it.
- Main offer and premium should share SAME primary features. Premium differs only on secondary features.
- The bigger the gasp, the more they'll buy.

"The only thing worse than making a $1,000 offer to a person with a $100 budget is making a $100 offer to someone with a $1,000 budget."

#### D. Rollover Upsells
"Credit some or all of previous purchase towards next offer."
4 situations: 1) Re-engage old customers (6+ months since last purchase). 2) Rescue upset customers (better than refund). 3) Rescue other people's upset customers (scrape negative reviews!). 4) Upsell regular customers.
- Price next offer at least 4x the credit (=25% max discount).
- Add urgency: make it a one-time offer. "Once in a customer lifetime."
- Gift card play: Sell $200 gift cards for $20 (90% off). They can only use on others. When recipients come in, roll gift card value towards next offer at 20% of price.

### 3. DOWNSELL OFFERS — What to offer when they say NO

**Rules of Downselling:**
- "No means no for THIS thing, not no for EVERYTHING."
- Downsells are trades: if you give something, get something.
- Personalize, don't pressure.
- NEVER drop price just to get someone to buy (customers talk). Change what they get, not the price for the same thing.
- It's less about having 100 products and more about having 100 ways to offer your product.

#### A. Payment Plan Downsells
7-Step Process (stop when they buy):
1. **Reward for paying in full** — "It's $15, but $10 if you prepay. You save $5." (vs. "It's $10, but $15 if you pay over time with $5 interest.")
2. **Third-party financing / credit card / layaway** — "Would you rather I decide your payment terms, or you decide?" (They pick credit card = you get paid today.)
3. **Half now, half later** — "When's the next time you get paid? Want to put half down today and the rest then?"
4. **Check if they still want it** — "Scale 1-10, how bad do you want this?" 8+ = continue. 7 or below = offer something different.
5. **Split into 3 payments** — 1/3 now, 1/3 next two paychecks.
6. **Evenly spread payments** — Over the full service duration.
7. **Free trial** — Covered separately as Trial with Penalty.

**Seesaw Downselling (simpler version):**
"Would you rather have giant monthly payments or tiny ones?" (Tiny.) "Normally it costs X. If you prepay today, you'll get a huge discount and zero monthly payments. That work?"

Key data: Monthly billing = 10.7% monthly churn. Quarterly = 5%. Annual = 2%.

#### B. Trial with Penalty
"If you do X, Y, Z, I'll let you start for free. If you don't, you pay."
- NOT "here's my thing, see if you like it." It's "here's my thing, get it free if you do this stuff that makes you a perfect fit for my next offer."
- 5 Steps: 1) Offer trial last (after all other offers refused). 2) Always get a card. 3) Always sell staying and paying upfront ("If this got you results, will you stay long-term?"). 4) Explain fees AFTER getting card. 5) Make check-ins required (upsell opportunities).
- If they like it → upsell longer-term or higher-value version.
- If they hate it → ask what they'd have liked different. Be angry at YOURSELF. Offer something else.
- If they didn't use it → reach out before they get billed. Offer to waive fee if they meet with you.
- Just call it a "free trial" — don't mention penalties in the name.

#### C. Feature Downsells
"Lower the price by changing what they get."
Formula: Take something away → lower the price → "How about now?"

Remove features from HIGHEST to LOWEST value. This lets customers re-upsell THEMSELVES.
- Remove something they LOVE + lower price a little = they take original offer (better deal).
- Remove something they HATE + lower price a lot = they take downsell (better deal).

**Feature categories to adjust:**
- Quantity: fewer sessions, less time, shorter duration, fewer items
- Quality: older versions, less reliable materials, lower social status materials
- Service Quality: response times, days of week, times of day, amount of time, location, cancellation policy, speed, ratio (1:1 vs 1:many), communication method, provider qualifications, live vs recorded, DIY/DWY/DFY, expiration, personalization
- Guarantees: remove guarantee entirely → customers realize its value → take original

**Temperature check after 2 downsells:** "Scale 1-10, how bad do you want this?" 8+ → payment plan. Under 7 → recombine features.

**Bartering:** "I'll knock $100 off if you: 1) leave reviews on all sites, 2) video testimonial, 3) public social posts showing progress, 4) introduce me to 2 friends. Deal?"
The advertising is worth more than $100 to you. To them, $100 savings > the advertising effort. Win-win.

### 4. CONTINUITY OFFERS — Keep them buying for good

"You can shear a sheep for a lifetime, but you can only skin it once."

Continuity provides ongoing value for ongoing payments. Trade-off: more customers but less cash today. Solution: make continuity offers LAST (after attraction → upsell → downsell).

#### A. Continuity Bonus Offers
"Get this awesome thing if you sign up today."
- Bonus typically has MORE value than first continuity payment.
- Advertise the BONUS, not the membership. "Get this free viable thing" > "Join my membership."
- Stack bonuses: 1 big thing + multiple smaller things. Mention individual dollar values.
- After they join, upsell bulk prepaid discount to boost 30-day profits.
- Make bonuses stuff you ALREADY have and do (past newsletters cost no extra time but are high value).
- Give titles after tenure milestones: Silver, Gold, Diamond. Customers care more about titles than bonuses.

**Pricing for continuity vs standalone (from testing thousands of gyms):**
| Target | Standalone Price vs Monthly |
|---|---|
| 50% choose continuity | 1.33x monthly rate |
| 60% choose continuity | 1.66x monthly rate |
| 70% choose continuity | 2x monthly rate |
| 80% choose continuity | 2.33x monthly rate |
| 90% choose continuity | 2.66x monthly rate |

#### B. Continuity Discount Offers
"Get X time free if you commit to Y time."
4 ways to apply the discount:
1. **Up front** — Free time first, paid time after. (Best for industries with enforced contracts: cell phones, real estate, equipment.)
2. **At the end** — Earn free time by making all payments on time.
3. **Spread over time** — Discount divided across term. Keep discount for life after term if all payments made.
4. **After first/second payment** — Collect cash before giving discount value.

**Critical pricing hack:** Bill weekly (every 4 weeks), not monthly. 13 four-week cycles vs 12 months = 8.3% more revenue. On 20% margin business, that's 41% profit increase. Just change a few words.

**Get second form of payment:** "Want to save the 3% processing fee? Give us a second form of payment." Prevents payment failures from expired/maxed cards.
**Get ACH** (direct bank) — cheapest way to transact besides cash.
**Gift cards for discounts** — Many people forget to use them. Full-price signup + referral tool.
**Lifetime discount at churn point** — "Stay past month 4, get lifetime lower rate."

**Cancellation policy:** Make fee equal to the discount they agreed to. "You got $600 in discounts by committing. You can cancel anytime by paying the $600."
**Exit interviews:** "I'll waive your cancellation fee if you come in and tell me what I could do better." Routinely save 1/3 to 1/2 of customers.

#### C. Waived Fee Offers
"Month-to-month with a big setup fee, OR I'll waive it if you commit to a year."
- Fee = typically 3-5x monthly rate.
- If they cancel inside commitment, they pay the waived fee.
- If they fulfill commitment, fee goes away forever.
- Larger fee = more commit. Smaller fee = more upfront cash.
- Best for commitments of 1 year+. Especially services taking long time (SEO, investing, weight loss).
- "Donation to cause they hate" option for extra motivation to stay.

### 5. MAKE YOUR MONEY MODEL — 3 Stages

**Stage 1: Get Cash** — Attraction offers get more customers for less.
**Stage 2: Get More Cash** — Upsell + Downsell offers make more from them faster.
**Stage 3: Get the Most Cash** — Continuity offers maximize total spend.

**Implementation Order (bootstrap businesses):**
1. Get customers reliably
2. Make sure they pay for themselves reliably
3. Make sure they pay for OTHER customers reliably
4. Start maximizing each customer's long-term value
5. Spend as many advertising dollars as possible to print money

**Build one stage at a time. Don't implement the full model at once — it will collapse.**
Perfect one offer → make it reliable → make it automatic → go to next stage.
Measure in quarters, not weeks.

### 6. REAL-WORLD MONEY MODEL EXAMPLES

**Gym Launch (Services, B2B):**
- Attraction: Decoy offer (free DIY vs $16K done-with-you)
- Upsell: Classic upsell ($42K/year advanced services, $36K prepaid)
- Downsell: Payment plan (start at $10K down, spread rest over 52 weeks, down to $800/week)
- Continuity: Menu upsell + Feature downsell (Full $800/wk → $500/wk → $300/wk → $200/wk → $100/wk minimum)

**Microgyms (Local Business):**
- Attraction: Win Your Money Back challenge
- Downsell: Payment plan + Trial with penalty
- Upsell: Menu upsell (supplement bundles)
- Downsell: Feature downsell (big bundle → small bundle → monthly subscription)
- Continuity: Rollover upsell + Lifetime discount ($50 off/month with 12-month commitment)

**Newsletter (Digital Product):**
- Attraction: Free trial ($0 → $399/month after 30 days)
- Upsell + Continuity: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later + Lifetime discount ($297 now, keep rate for life)

**Dog Food (Physical Product):**
- Attraction: Buy X Get Y Free (buy 4 months, get 2 free)
- Upsell: Classic upsell (toys, vitamins)
- Downsell: Feature downsell ("Just the premium food then, you don't want anything else do you?")
- Continuity: Automatic renewal after block purchase

### 7. HOW TO BUILD YOUR MONEY MODEL

**Step 1:** Pick an attraction offer. Advertise it. Get leads → customers. May take up to a year.
**Step 2:** Pick an upsell offer that solves the NEXT problem your attraction offer creates.
**Step 3:** Pick a downsell offer for the people who say no. More total cash from same leads.
**Step 4:** Pick a continuity offer. One last sale in 30-day window + recurring cash.

**Key principles:**
- It's less about 100 products, more about 100 ways to offer your product
- Price new offers LOW at first. Get yeses, improve product, then raise prices.
- Affiliate products can fill gaps in your money model without delivery headaches.
- Turn attraction offers into continuity with automatic renewal (2-for-1).
- You can mix and match offers however you want. The categories are how Alex uses them — many were learned from people who used them differently.
- Simple scales. Fancy fails.

**Bootstrap businesses must get customers at a profit.**
If you can't cover cost of getting + servicing a customer within 30 days, you run out of cash before you have a shot.
