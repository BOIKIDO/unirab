# Branding Agent — Knowledge Base
Source: $100M Branding Playbook by Alex Hormozi (36 pages)

## Core Definition
Branding = pairing two or more things together.
- Good branding = pairing your business with something the majority of your ideal audience LIKES
- Bad branding = pairing your business with something the majority of your audience DISLIKES
- No branding = exposure results in them treating you as invisible
- Advertising = letting people know about your stuff. Branding = WHAT you let them know.

## Brand Measurement Framework (3 Variables)
1. **Reach** — How many people know about your stuff (output of advertising). Primary element of a brand.
2. **Influence** — The chance it changes behavior when they see it.
3. **Direction** — How behavior changes: towards (buy) or away (reject).

## Advertising × Branding Grid (4 Quadrants)
- Good advertising + Good branding = more people know + they like it → WIN
- Good advertising + Bad branding = more people know + they HATE it → Pepsi/Kendall Jenner disaster
- Bad advertising + Good branding = few people know + they like it → hidden gem
- Bad advertising + Bad branding = few people know + they hate it → nothing happening

## "Any Publicity" Truth
- Good beats bad. Bad beats none. Lots of good beats everything.
- The saying should be: "Any publicity is better than no publicity, but good publicity is better than bad."
- You don't need to be polarizing to build a strong brand (Taylor Swift, Apple, Mother Teresa).

## How Branding Translates to Sales (8-Step Process)
1. Start with a brand that means nothing
2. Pair it with stuff your customers like
3. Brand starts to mean "thing the customer likes" to them
4. Buy a generic product
5. Add your brand → transforms into premium branded product
6. Customers want to associate with the positively-associated branded product
7. They exchange money for your premium product
8. You get higher profits from higher prices because of those associations

## Branded vs Unbranded Economics
- Branded: Higher CTR (3% vs 1%), higher conversion (6% vs 4%), higher price ($5,500 vs $2,500)
- Result: 4.5x more sales, 11x higher ROAS
- "If you've got the power to raise prices without losing business to a competitor, you've got a very good business." — Warren Buffett

## 4 Steps to Start/Grow/Pivot a Brand
### Step 1: Figure Out Ideal Customer (4 Criteria)
- Growing market
- Has money (can afford your stuff)
- Easy to target
- In pain (desperately wants what you sell)

### Step 2: Figure Out What They Like
List everything they want, struggle with, and value.

### Step 3: Associate Your Brand With Relevant Things They Like
- Make content → Distribute it → Iterate based on audience response
- Give away valuable free stuff (videos, blogs, articles, booklets, PDFs)

### Step 4: Optimize Associations for Max Profit
5 directional pivots: Up market, Down market, Adjacent, Broader, Narrower

## The Bouquet Metaphor
- Brand = bouquet of flowers (products, values, experiences, people tied together)
- Narrow brand = only red roses
- Broader brand = adding different flowers
- One ugly flower (bad pairing) messes up the whole bouquet
- Recovery: overwhelm with good associations until the bad one shrinks into irrelevance

## Levels of Authority — The Branding Cycle
1. **What We Say** (content, ads, outreach) — least influential
2. **What Other People Say** (affiliates, customers) — more influential
3. **What They Experience Themselves** — most authoritative

The cycle: Advertising → First sale → Product experience → Brand association → Repeat purchase + Word of mouth

## Brand Pivots — Net Audience Change
- Gain more than you lose = good trade
- Lose more than you gain = bad trade
- Losing audience is part of life. Don't let 5 mean comments stop you from gaining 500 new people.

## Branding Mistakes Recovery
- One bad pairing hurts the brand (like one rotten flower in a bouquet)
- Don't try to eliminate the bad association — overwhelm it with more good ones
- Example: Kanye West — said things people don't like, but products people love overwhelm the bad

## Long-Term Branding ROI
When done right: Higher CTR, higher closing rates, higher prices, repeat purchases, cheaper acquisition, larger customers over time, compounding word of mouth, attracts best talent, builds an asset with intrinsic value.

When done wrong: Ward off good customers, attract bad ones, pay more for customers, lose customers on back end, always need new crazy offers, can't attract talent, constantly consider changing businesses.
