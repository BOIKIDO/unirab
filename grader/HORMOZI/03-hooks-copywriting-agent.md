# Hooks & Copywriting Agent — Knowledge Base
Source: $100M Hooks Playbook by Alex Hormozi (26 pages)

## Core Definition
A hook is whatever gets the attention of your audience FIRST — first few seconds of video, first line of text, first image they see.
Good hook = sells the person on paying attention. Makes them think "Oh, this is for me."

## Why Hooks Matter
"After you've written your headline, you've spent eighty cents of your advertising dollar." — David Ogilvy

Mastering hooks can 5x, 10x, or 100x your business without changing anything else.
If you have 10 hours to advertise, spend the first 8 hours on the hooks.

## The Dean Graziosi / Larry King Story
Changed ONLY the opening hook on a failed infomercial. Everything else identical — book, cover, price, website, offer. The new hook: "Have you ever in your adult life looked at yourself in the mirror and thought 'I should be further ahead by now?'" → Became a monster that sold millions.

## How Hooks Work — Two Parts
1. **Call Out** — Gets the prospect to say "This is for me" (verbal + nonverbal). Harnesses the "cocktail party effect" — cutting through noise.
2. **Condition for Value** — The hook itself. Promises value if they consume. "If [TARGET] [CONDITION] [THEY WILL GET VALUE/REWARD]"

## Types of Verbal Hooks
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Labels | "Local business owners, I have a gift for you" |
| Questions (Yes) | "Would you pay $1,000 to have the business of your dreams in 30 days?" |
| Questions (Open) | "Which would you rather be?" |
| Conditionals | "If you're working all the time and your business isn't growing, you're working on the wrong sh*t." |
| Commands | "Read this if you're tired of being broke." |
| Statements | "The smartest thing you can do today..." |
| Lists/Steps | "In this video I'm going to talk about the 28 ways to stay poor." |
| Narratives | "One day I was in the back and this old lady comes in and she was piss angry." |
| Exclamations/Provocative | "Ahhhhh… This is the blueprint to becoming a millionaire." |

## Non-Verbal Hooks
Visuals (images, motion, contrast) and sounds (unexpected noises, music, silence).
Like the cocktail party — a dropped tray OR a champagne flute *cling* both get attention for different reasons. Everyone still wants to know what happens next.

## 70-20-10 Hook Creation Rule (Google's Innovation Model)
- **70% — Core:** Proven hooks from your own best content. "More of what works."
- **20% — Emerging:** "Winner adjacent" — model concepts from other niches.
- **10% — New Ideas:** Crazy experiments. Document losses, scale winners.

## Hook Creation System
1. Create spreadsheet per platform: Name, Hook, Views, Link
2. Review best hooks before every content session
3. Subscribe to outlier-detection software tools
4. Search platforms by topic for highest-performing content
5. Paste outliers into slide deck with ideas for repurposing

## Testing Hooks
- For ads: Record 10 hooks for every 1 piece of content (10x ratio)
- For short content: Reuse winning tweets as hooks, expand in the content
- For long content: Record 3-4 hooks, let team pick best in post-production
- For emails: Run active split tests on every campaign, document winners

## Starting from Zero
"Make a bunch with no idea what's gonna happen. See what happened. Follow 70-20-10. Top 10% performers → make more like that. Bottom 10% → make less. Repeat until the end of time. Let the data do the teaching."

## Hook Checklist
First time: Look at top-performing content → Isolate hooks → Write down 50 favorites → Use all of them.
Every time after: Top 10% performers → 70% using top hooks → 20% permutations → 10% completely new.

## Good Hooks Contain
- Some way to catch attention (call out)
- Some implication of value if they consume it

- More clicks on content, ads, outbound → more revenue
